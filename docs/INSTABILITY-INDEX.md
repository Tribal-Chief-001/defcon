# Defcon Instability Index (DII): design spec

Status: **design, not built** (written 2026-10-18 after the owner liked World Monitor's Country Instability Index and asked to flesh the idea out). It supersedes the provisional 7-day "tension index" in `docs/TENSION-INDEX.md` once built; until then that index stays as it is. Method tags as in `docs/RESEARCH-v1.1.md`: **[read]**, **[search]**, **[claim]**, **[unverified]**, **[design]** (our choice).

## 1. Why this index, and why ours can be better than the one people like

World Monitor's CII (v8) **[read: its methodology pages]**: a 0 to 100 score for **31 hand-picked countries**; `eventScore = 0.25 Unrest + 0.30 Conflict + 0.20 Security + 0.25 Information`, then `0.40 baselineRisk + 0.60 eventScore + boosts` (up to ten add-ons: climate, cyber, wildfire, travel advisory, displacement, earthquake, sanctions, ship disruption, and more) with floors for UCDP war and advisories. Bands: Low 0 to 30, Normal 31 to 50, Elevated 51 to 65, High 66 to 80, Critical 81 to 100. The page says plainly: **"These weights are editorial, not empirical"**, there are **no backtests for CII**, per-country baselines and "event multipliers" (0.3 for the US up to 3.0 for North Korea) are **hand set**, there are **no confidence intervals**, and attribution uses "a rectangular approximation" of borders. It is called "a triage dial, not a verdict".

What people like about it, and we keep: **one glanceable number per country, bands, a 24-hour movement arrow, a roll-up, and a breakdown by component.**

Where Defcon can be better, all of it fitting what the project already stands for:

| World Monitor CII | Defcon DII (design) |
|---|---|
| 31 curated countries | **All countries**, with a confidence flag instead of omission |
| Hand-set weights and per-country multipliers | **Weights learned from data and published**; no per-country editorial constants |
| No validation published | **Backtest published and regenerated by a script** (hold-out years, calibration plot, baselines) |
| One blended number | **Separate constructs** (risk, momentum, activity now) shown together, never silently mixed |
| No uncertainty | **Confidence and a range** on every score; low-coverage countries say so |
| Dozens of ad hoc boosts | Few, justified inputs; shocks (quakes, fires, outages) shown as **separate indicators**, not folded into "instability" |
| News velocity as a component | **No news-volume component** (it measures attention, not instability); shown as its own context |
| Closed methodology versions in prose | `methodology_version`, a changelog, and a reproducible training script in the repo |

## 2. What the index means (constructs)

"Instability" is not one thing; mixing causes, symptoms and attention in one number is how indices become unfalsifiable. The DII keeps **three constructs**, each valid on its own, and one headline that combines only the first two:

1. **Risk (structural + history).** *How likely is a violent escalation in this country in the next 3 months, given who and where it is?* Slow-moving inputs plus recent violence history. This is the **trained, backtested** model and the **headline** number.
2. **Momentum.** *Is violence rising or falling compared with this country's own recent baseline?* A signed change, 28 days against the previous 90.
3. **Activity now.** *What is being reported in the last 7 days?* The existing tension logic (UCDP human-coded plus GDELT auto-coded at half weight, with the `comparable` rule). Fast, noisy, shown beside the headline with its own confidence, **not** averaged into it.

**Confidence** (High / Medium / Low) is the fourth thing and travels with every number.

Headline = `Risk` (0 to 100, a calibrated probability scaled, see section 4) with a Momentum arrow. Activity now is displayed as a second meter. A country can be "high risk, quiet now" or "low risk, spiking now": both are informative and a single blend would hide it.

## 3. Inputs and their licences

Only open, citable inputs. Licence status is **[unverified]** unless stated; each must be confirmed before ingestion (this is a task, section 9).

| Input | Role | Source | Licence / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fatalities and events per country-month, state-based + non-state + one-sided | Violence history (the label for training too) | UCDP GED, already ingested | CC BY 4.0, in use |
| Recent events (30 d, 7 d) | Momentum, Activity now | UCDP candidate (human) + GDELT (auto) | in use |
| Infant mortality | Structural (a classic predictor **[search]**: PITF) | World Bank WDI `SP.DYN.IMRT.IN` | CC BY 4.0 (same API as the dossiers) |
| Governance: political stability, government effectiveness, rule of law, voice and accountability | Structural (regime quality) | World Bank WGI | **[unverified]** licence, annual with about a year of lag |
| Years since the last conflict / conflict history | Structural | UCDP GED | in use |
| **Neighbours in conflict** | "Bad neighbourhood" effect **[search]**: PITF uses four or more bordering states in conflict | Our own India-POV border graph (`neighbours` in `countries.json`) | in the repo |
| Displacement | Structural stress | UNHCR Refugee Data Finder API (open, keyless **[search]**) | **[unverified]** attribution terms |
| Population (for rates) | Normalisation | WDI | CC BY 4.0 |
| Regime type | Structural | V-Dem or WGI proxy | V-Dem licence **[unverified]**; WGI proxy avoids it |

**Deliberately excluded:** news-volume or sentiment (attention, not instability), ACLED (licence, see `docs/RESEARCH-v1.1.md` 5a), OpenSanctions (non-commercial), travel advisories as an input (editorial; can be shown as a reference line), per-country editorial multipliers, anything we cannot cite.

## 4. Model

- **Target (label)** for training: *escalation within 90 days* = UCDP fatalities in the next 90 days ≥ 25 (a "meaningful violence" line), and a second target, *rise*: next-90-day fatalities ≥ 2x the previous 90 days **and** ≥ 25. Both are defined on country-months so they can be computed for 1989 to 2025 from the data we already hold. **[design]**: thresholds to be validated by sensitivity analysis (try 10, 25, 100).
- **Model:** regularised **logistic regression** (interpretable, like the PITF minimal models **[search]**: a 2021 study built minimal logistic models from regime type, infant mortality and stability years). Features are few: log fatalities in the last 3, 12 and 36 months, months since the last violent month, infant mortality (log), governance score, neighbours in conflict (count and share), displacement per capita (log), population (log). No hand-set constants.
- **Score:** predicted probability `p`; displayed as `score = 100 * rank-calibrated(p)` so bands mean something stable: bands are defined **by calibrated probability** (for example Low under 2 %, Elevated 2 to 8 %, High 8 to 25 %, Critical over 25 %, thresholds fixed from the validation set and published), not by arbitrary cut points. **[design]** The headline also shows the plain probability ("about 12 % chance of 25+ conflict deaths in the next 3 months").
- **Momentum:** signed, `log1p(fatalities_28d) - log1p(fatalities_prev_90d / 3.2)`-style ratio against the country's own baseline, clipped; arrow thresholds fixed; "no data" is not zero.
- **Activity now:** unchanged from `TENSION-INDEX.md` (weights, half weight for auto-coded, `comparable` flag), moved to a secondary meter.
- **Uncertainty:** (a) a **range** from bootstrapping the training data (or a Bayesian logistic with a Laplace approximation), shown as a bar; (b) **confidence** (High / Medium / Low) from data coverage: share of inputs observed vs imputed, age of the newest structural data, whether human-coded events exist in the last 60 days, whether the country is flagged as under-reported (English-language and media-access bias **[search]**: GDELT and ACLED under-index low-media-access places). Low confidence widens the range and says why.
- **Missing structural data:** imputed from region and income group medians **and tagged imputed** (never silent). A country with most inputs imputed is Low confidence.

## 5. Backtest and validation (what makes it credible)

A script (`scripts/index/backtest.ts`, regenerates `docs/INSTABILITY-BACKTEST.md`) reports:

1. **Splits by time, not at random:** train through 2016, validate 2017 to 2020, **test 2021 to 2025** (includes Ukraine 2022, Sudan 2023, Gaza 2023: regime shifts that stress a model). Report the test years separately, plus a rolling-origin version.
2. **Baselines it must beat:** (a) persistence ("last 90 days' fatalities"), (b) "any conflict in the last 12 months", (c) a prior-year-fatalities-only logistic, (d) the old provisional tension score recomputed on history.
3. **Metrics for rare events:** area under the precision-recall curve (not just ROC), Brier score and its decomposition, a **calibration plot** (predicted vs observed by decile), top-k hit rate ("of the 20 highest-risk countries each quarter, how many saw escalation?"), recall at a fixed alert budget, and **lead time**.
4. **Honesty checks:** confidence intervals by resampling countries (clustered), performance split by region and by confidence level, a list of the **biggest misses and false alarms** printed in the report, and a **sensitivity table** (thresholds, windows, dropped features).
5. **Never** claim causation or prediction of a specific event; the page says "a calibrated probability from historical patterns, not a forecast of what will happen". It is compared (qualitatively) with the open VIEWS forecasts **[search]** where licence allows, as an external reference, not an input.
6. **Reproducibility:** the training script, the feature table builder and the fitted coefficients (a small JSON) are committed; `methodology_version` and a changelog travel with every published score.

## 6. Product design

**Where it appears**
- **Country dossier:** a hero card: the score (large numeral), band chip, probability sentence, momentum arrow, confidence chip, the uncertainty bar; below it a **"why this score"** list: each feature's contribution (coefficient x standardised value, so it is exactly additive on the log-odds scale) with plain-language labels ("conflict deaths in the last 12 months: raises risk", "neighbours in conflict: 3 of 5"); a **5-year monthly sparkline** from the backtest series; a link to the method.
- **Globe layer ("Instability"):** a country choropleth on the globe (fill from band colour at low alpha, hairline borders stay), toggled in the controls row; legend with bands and the confidence hatch for Low-confidence countries (never fully filled).
- **Rails:** a third rail or a switch on Hotspots: "Highest risk", "Biggest movers (momentum)", "Quiet but high-risk" (the interesting list: high Risk, low Activity now).
- **Compare:** pick two or three countries side by side (score, components, history).
- **Global roll-up:** one number is **not** shown by default (it is the weakest idea in this family); instead a distribution strip ("how many countries per band") and the top movers.
- **Time travel:** the scrubber moves the **historical** index (precomputed monthly series), so "what did the index say before 2022?" is one drag: this is the showcase.
- **Method panel** (in About and per country): inputs with sources and dates, weights/coefficients, backtest summary, limitations, version.

**Design language fit:** hairline gauge (a 270-degree arc drawn with a 1 px stroke and a filled tick), mono labels, bone numerals, lime for selection, orange only for High and Critical, a redaction bar for "unavailable", confidence as a thin 3-segment indicator. No gradients, no shadows, WCAG AA.

**Honesty rules (hard):** a score never appears without its confidence; Low-confidence countries show a range and "limited data" instead of a confident band; imputed inputs are labelled; the time of the newest input is shown; wording is "risk estimate", never "will".

## 7. Engineering plan (small tasks, each testable)

1. **Feature table builder** (`scripts/index/features.ts`): country-month panel 1989 to now from UCDP GED + WDI (+ WGI, UNHCR if licences allow) + neighbours; golden tests on a few countries.
2. **Label builder** and **dataset checks** (no leakage: features use data available at the prediction month, with the published release lags modelled).
3. **Trainer** (`scripts/index/train.ts`): regularised logistic regression in TypeScript (IRLS or gradient descent, deterministic, no new dependency), cross-validation by year, bootstrap ranges; writes `data/index/model.json` (coefficients, scalers, thresholds, version).
4. **Backtest report generator** with the metrics above; the report is committed and a test fails if calibration degrades past a stated tolerance after a retrain.
5. **Scorer** (`src/index/score.ts`, pure, tested): features of the live data -> score, probability, range, confidence, contributions; handles missing inputs and imputation tags.
6. **Monthly series and daily snapshot publisher**: `public/data/index/<ISO>.json` (history and current), size budget in `check-budgets`.
7. **UI:** dossier hero card + "why this score", methodology panel, then the globe layer, rails and compare view; axe, keyboard and contrast tests as for the rest of the UI.
8. **Docs:** replace `TENSION-INDEX.md` with this spec once built; DECISIONS entries for every threshold.

Rough size: tasks 1 to 4 are the real work (data engineering and honest evaluation); 5 to 7 reuse the existing UI and pipeline patterns.

## 8. Risks and open questions (for the owner)

- **It can be wrong, loudly.** Models trained on 1989 to 2025 may miss regime shifts; the backtest report must be public and the UI must say "estimate". If it fails the baselines, we do not ship it as a headline.
- **Licences** of WGI, UNHCR and any V-Dem use must be confirmed first; fallback is WDI-only structural features.
- **GDELT/English bias** and **low media access** make Low-confidence countries common; that is correct, but the UI must make Low confidence look like honesty, not like a bug.
- **Naming:** "Instability Index" can sound alarmist; alternatives: "Violence Risk Index". Decision needed. **Defcon-style** wording must remain clear that it is not the US DEFCON level.
- **Scope choice:** ship Risk + Momentum first and leave "Activity now" as the existing tension; or fold Activity now into the dossier hero from day one.
- **Political sensitivity:** ranking countries (including India and its neighbours) invites scrutiny; the method panel and the public backtest are the defence, and map policy stays as documented.

## 9. First step

Before any modelling: (a) confirm licences and update cadence of WGI and UNHCR; (b) build the country-month feature table and look at it (coverage, gaps, leakage); (c) fit the simplest model, **a prior-violence-only baseline**, and see how hard it is to beat. If structural features add nothing measurable over persistence, say so and ship Momentum and Activity now only, with the finding written down. That honesty is the point of building it ourselves.
